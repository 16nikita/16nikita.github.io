I am a naturally inquisitive person, with a childlike instinct to always follow up with *why?* Research has always felt like second nature to me, though my official journey in academia began when I was 20 years old. I attended Cornell University, I fell in love with plant research upon first sight. Plants are funky and are constantly doing crazy things that subvert our expectations in science. What I find so interesting is that, despite being rooted in place, plants are remarkably dynamic: they can sense and respond to their environments, and they have evolved ingenious ways to compete and cooperate with their neighbors and thrive in the conditions they were born into. The more I have learned about plants, the more I came to understand all the ways they are just as important in the web of life as any other organism. <strong style="color:#2E6F3E;">They sustain ecosystems, shape our environment, and provide us sustenance.</strong>.  As a vegetarian, they are the reason I am alive. Plant scientists have helped discover fundamental biological processes that echo far beyond their field. For me, studying plants is intellectually exciting and deeply consequential: understanding how they function, adapt, and evolve can teach us fundamental biology while helping us confront challenges such as food insecurity, biodiversity loss, and climate change.

I worked in the Pawlowski Lab on a series of projects using computational approaches to illuminate the factors controlling meiotic recombination in maize and rice. These efforts were directed toward uncovering the mechanisms governing recombination to help crops better harness genetic diversity in the face of growing climate pressures. During my time in this lab, I found myself gravitating towards using computational approaches to answer our burning questions, and I began to tailor my education as such. I graduated *summa cum laude* from Cornell University in December 2022 with a B.A. in Biological Sciences, concentrating in Computational Biology and minoring in Climate Change, and then I moved to San Francisco to keep growing and evolving as a computational scientist.

Currently, I work as a data scientist in the [Wang Lab](https://brucewanglab.ucsf.edu/) at the University of California, San Francisco (UCSF). Here, I specialize in molecular genetics techniques, specifically in single-cell RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, and multi-omics pipeline development and deployment. Our lab aims to understand how cellular differences contribute to heterogeneity in function and in disease responses in human and mice livers. We also work towards defining a physiological baseline for mouse livers, which hopefully translates to human livers, too. Simultaneously, I serve as a bioinformatics collaborator for all labs within the UCSF Liver Center community.

Through these experiences, I have amassed a wealth of computational skills, and more importantly, seen the consequences of social and environmental stressors on both human and non-human beings. However, despite working across different model systems, plants have lingered in my mind, and I knew I would want to return to plant research at some point in my career. Moving forward, I plan to integrate my interests in plant genomics and bioinformatics to fight for environmental justice. My future career goals include earning a PhD in Plant Biology in efforts to follow this dream and eventually becoming a resident scientist at a plant conservation center somewhere. 

Thanks for taking the time to visit my website!

<h2 style="color:#2E6F3E;">What am I good at?🌟</h2>

**Programming & Pipelines:** Python, R, Bash, and Java; building reproducible workflows for large-scale genomic and multi-omic datasets.

**Single-Cell & Spatial Genomics:** Analysis of scRNA-seq, snRNA-seq, and spatial transcriptomics data, including quality control, clustering, cell-type annotation, differential expression, trajectory inference, cell–cell communication, and spatial analysis using Seurat, Scanpy, Squidpy, and CellChat.

**Epigenomics & Sequencing Analysis:** Processing and analysis of ATAC-seq and other sequencing data, including alignment, peak calling, quantification, and QC using Bowtie2, STAR, Samtools, MACS3, featureCounts, HOMER, and deepTools.

**Multi-omics & Statistical Analysis:** Integrating transcriptomic, epigenomic, and proteomic data; differential and pathway analysis; KEGG/GO enrichment; batch correction; mixed-effects modeling; and machine-learning approaches.

**Statistical Computing & Visualization:** Creating interpretable, publication-ready analyses and visualizations with R/tidyverse, ggplot2, Python, NumPy, and pandas.

**High-Performance Computing:** Working in Unix/Linux and HPC environments using SLURM, Git, conda, cloud workflows, and scalable approaches for managing large biological datasets.
