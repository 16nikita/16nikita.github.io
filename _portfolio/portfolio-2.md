**When:** May 2024-Ongoing

**Where:** Bruce Wang Lab, UCSF Liver Center

Circadian rhythms, metabolic state, and sexual maturation critically influence liver function, yet baseline transcriptional regulation across these axes remains poorly understood. We modeled wild-type liver physiology in mice and applied a Python-based single-cell analysis pipeline to characterize hepatocyte and non-parenchymal cell transcriptional states under circadian, fasting, and estrous cycle conditions.

Our analyses revealed coordinated transcriptional programs: key heme biosynthesis genes (Alas1, Alas2) exhibited time-dependent oscillations, with sex-specific differences in Alas1 expression indicating circadian regulation influenced by hormonal status. Fasting induced conserved transcriptional responses across liver cell types, including upregulation of mitochondrial genes supporting electron transport chain activity and ribosomal RNA expression to maintain metabolic homeostasis. Estrous cycle analysis further revealed state-specific modulation of heme pathway genes between cycling and non-cycling females.

These results provide a high-resolution map of physiological liver regulation and identify cell types most susceptible to transcriptional dysregulation, establishing a framework for understanding disease vulnerability across circadian, metabolic, and hormonal contexts.
