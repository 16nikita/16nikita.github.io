**When:** May 2024-Ongoing

**Where:** Bruce Wang Lab, UCSF Liver Center

Circadian rhythms, liver metabolism, and stages of sexual maturation influence disease manifestation, yet baseline liver physiology along these axes remains poorly characterized. To address this gap, we modeled wild-type liver physiology in mice, focusing on the effects of circadian rhythms, fasting, and the estrous cycle on cellular transcriptional states. We refined a Python-based single-cell analysis pipeline to characterize transcriptional changes across physiological conditions and identify cell types most susceptible to dysregulation. 

Our analyses revealed coordinated transcriptional programs: key heme biosynthesis genes, Alas1 and Alas2, exhibited time-dependent oscillations, indicating rhythmic regulation of the heme pathway critical for aerobic metabolism and disrupted in disorders such as porphyria cutanea tarda. Alas1 patterns also differed between sexes, highlighting sex-dependent circadian regulation relevant to disease susceptibility. Fasting induced a conserved transcriptional signature across liver cell types, marked by upregulation of mitochondrial genes supporting electron transport chain activity and elevated ribosomal RNA expression for metabolic homeostasis. Finally, estrous cycle analyses revealed state-specific variation in Alas1 expression between cycling and non-cycling females, suggesting hormonal modulation of heme production. These findings provide a high-resolution map of physiological liver regulation and offer a framework for understanding disease vulnerability across circadian, metabolic, and hormonal contexts.
