**When:** July 2023-Ongoing

**Where:** Bruce Wang Lab, UCSF Liver Center

Porphyria cutanea tarda (PCT) is a liver disorder characterized by dysregulated heme biosynthesis, yet the cellular mechanisms driving hepatocyte dysfunction remain poorly understood. We performed single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis on wild-type (WT) and UROD-treated mouse hepatocytes, integrating both 10X Genomics and SeqWell datasets using scVI to capture diverse cellular states while mitigating batch effects. Differential gene expression analysis revealed that UROD-treated hepatocytes exhibit upregulation of stress-response pathways, necrosis, and immunogenic signaling, with concomitant downregulation of ferroptosis pathways in polyploid hepatocytes.

Notably, UROD treatment induces polyploidization, particularly within cells displaying elevated insulin pathway activity, consistent with PI3K–AKT–mTOR-mediated cytokinesis failure as a potential mechanism. Zonation markers (Cyp2e1, Cyp2f2) were coexpressed in a higher proportion of UROD-treated hepatocytes, indicating partial disruption of normal lobular zonation. Pseudotime trajectory analysis using Monocle3 suggested dynamic transitions between clusters, highlighting adaptive responses in subsets of hepatocytes and progressive stress accumulation in others.

Pathway enrichment across clusters further supported these findings, with upregulation of ribosomal, oxidative phosphorylation, and ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis pathways, and downregulation of metabolic and blood coagulation functions in treated cells. Integration of QC-filtered datasets confirmed that observed effects were not artifacts of selective cell loss, reinforcing the robustness of the polyploid and stress-response signatures.

Together, these analyses provide a comprehensive single-cell characterization of hepatocyte responses to UROD treatment, revealing polyploidization, stress signaling, and disruption of zonation as hallmarks of early PCT pathology. This work is currently being prepared for manuscript submission.
